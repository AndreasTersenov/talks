## The idea

The slide keeps its mechanism: the chain, one click for the claim, four clicks for the questions,
each lighting its step. What changes is the words. Nothing on this slide needs to be precise; the
room needs a glimpse of what each part is about. Each card names the step in its tab and asks a
one-line question in words that need no vocabulary beyond what the spoken walk gives. The terms
are taught in the parts, as `STRUCTURE.md` §"Where each concept is taught" already says.

## The script, A0.8

<!-- script-start -->
Here is the same thing, reduced to the steps I will talk about. Galaxy shapes go in. From the shapes we make a map of the mass distribution. From the map we take a few numbers, the summary statistics. We compare those with simulations, and out come the parameters.

[CLICK] **▲** Now, the word trustworthy in the title. Every one of these steps can fail without any sign of it at the end. A biased mass map, a statistic that throws information away, a simulation that misses some physics, all of them give a posterior that looks just as good. So trustworthy means three concrete things. The parameters are unbiased, the error bars are right, and the result does not depend on physics we could not model. The four questions test exactly that.

[CLICK] One, the map. Several algorithms make it. **▲** Does the choice matter for the cosmological results?

[CLICK] Two, same step. **▲** Can we build one algorithm that is accurate, fast, and comes with error bars we can trust, for a survey the size of Euclid?

[CLICK] Three, the summary statistics. A map is too big to compare with theory, so we reduce it to a few numbers. **▲** How much of the cosmological information do those numbers keep, and does it take a neural network to keep it?

[CLICK] Four, the simulations. They leave out astrophysics we cannot model, and it affects the small scales. **▲** Once we remove those scales, do the statistics still constrain the parameters better than the standard analysis?

Two questions about the map, two about the summary statistics. This picture comes back at the head of every part with one box lit, and we come back to these four at the end, with the answers.
